**Flags**

- `LOW`: under 800 px on the long edge, so it can't go full-bleed on desktop.
- `FRAME`: an instant-print border is baked in. Crop it or embrace it.
- `TEXT`: typography is baked in. It can't be cropped freely and needs its own alt text.
- `MOCKUP`: a design mockup, not a photograph of a real piece.

## Art-director notes

**Strongest full-bleed images**
- the six collages (about 3277×4096)
- `polaroid-10` (group on a lawn, landscape) and `polaroid-17` (gathering with a guitar, landscape)
- `polaroid-04` (heads together in a circle)
- `polaroid-20/21` (sunlit portraits)
- `striped-tee_04` (group in a showroom)
- `grey-sweatsuit-01/02` (flat-lays, about 3072×4096)
- `before-after-sweaters-02`
- `banner_04`

**How to treat each group**
- **Instant-frame images** (lifestyle, `polaroid-22…31`, most products): show them as *objects*, at real print proportion. Never crop them inside a box: the frame is the brand's real medium.
- **The `-nov26` product set** (24 files at 360×540): good subjects (worn shots, studio, Polaroid piles), but sharp only up to about 180 CSS px on a retina screen. **Ask for the originals.** Until then they appear small in galleries and never as heroes.
- **Hoodie MOCKUP files:** always labelled "design preview" on screen, as striped.world does.
- **Faces:** the archive shows real, unnamed people. No names and no captions beyond what the photo shows.

**Gaps**
- no founder portrait intended as one
- no video files
- no vector wordmark
- no photos for 2 of the 9 pieces
- no behind-the-scenes of distressing in progress (only before and after)
