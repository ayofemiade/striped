## 13. Current website: brutal audit

**Method.** Rendered every page at 1440×900, with full-page captures in [research/current-site/](research/current-site/) and phones at 390×844. Computed styles and colours with `getComputedStyle`.

**Verdict.** It's an honest, tidy and accessible small-shop site: the copy is careful, the preorder rules are clear, there's a skip link and the tabs work with the keyboard. But it's dressed as a template. It makes a world-building brand look like any Inter-on-white store. The site's most distinctive assets (the instant prints, the scrawled wordmark, the collages, the before/after process, a musician who performs in clothing shops) are either boxed small or not on the site at all.

**Numbers**
- Homepage: 2,831 px tall (3.1 screens desktop, 3.4 on phone). 4 content sections.
- 1 font family (Inter), **28 distinct text styles** on one page.
- Blue `#0055B5` used for nearly every text, link, rule and button, so the blue signals nothing in particular.
- Contrast passes (7.08:1 blue on white).
- No horizontal overflow at 390 px.

**What's wrong**
1. **The world is hidden.** "The world" (music, performances, 31 photos, 19 videos) sits behind a signup wall, so a first-time visitor sees none of it. The best brand content is the least visible.
2. **No story.** The About page is one paragraph and three photos. The process (thrift → distress → patch → embroider) lives only on Instagram.
3. **Photography boxed small.** Instant prints sit inside a cream card inside a grid cell (product page). The best image on the home page (banner_04) is split 60/40 with a text panel.
4. **The type has no conviction.** Inter only, at 28 sizes and weights, while the brand's own posters use a condensed black grotesk, a typewriter face and a hand-scrawled wordmark.
5. **Music and clothes are separate rooms.** No page says what the brand actually does: the music happens where the clothes are.
6. **Shop without rhythm.** Every card has the same skeleton. "coming soon" tiles are empty cream boxes.
7. **No credits.** Collaborators (Grounded, Nela Prints, Run It Wave, Public Estrellas, @zarasride) appear only on Instagram, yet they *are* the story of "made in LA".

**Keep**
- All honest preorder wording, verbatim.
- The ↗ arrow, lowercase voice, "1/1" language, `#0055B5`, cream, the tee logo.
- The product data and photos.
- The member idea (music for fans), done better.

**Discard**
- Inter.
- The boxed card grid.
- The cream "coming soon" tiles.
- The hidden-by-default world.

## 14. Opportunities

1. **Make the stripe the structure,** not a decoration. "Every stripe has a story" is the brand's own line.
2. Put the **process** (before → after) on the site, using their own photos and captions.
3. Show **music in the rooms where the clothes are** (Playoffs Vintage, Grounded Showroom) as one story, not two tabs.
4. Use the **instant print** as the brand's image unit, at full scale, not boxed.
5. Show the **31-photo archive** in public as "the people", keeping the music itself for members.
6. **Credit the makers** of LA. It's a strong, true "made in LA" story.
7. A shop where **1/1 is the hero**: "this one, not one like it".

## 17. Must not be assumed or claimed

- No invented numbers, testimonials, press, stockists, sales counts or follower counts.
- **Sustainability:** the bio says "100% sustainable" and the patch says "100% recycled". These are the brand's claims, not ours. The demo may quote "100% recycled" as the patch's own caption. It **must not** state "100% sustainable" as a site-wide fact, or any environmental statistic, without approval (see the FTC Green Guides risk in 03). Default wording is factual: "thrifted sweaters, hand-distressed blanks, patches cut from old denim".
- No payment, checkout, stock, shipping dates or reservation. The interest list stays unpaid.
- No dates or places of shows beyond the feed titles. No tour claims (the highlight "t.e.h. tour" isn't explained).
- Not "approved by" or "in partnership with" STRIPED. The demo carries a discreet "concept by MoyoCode" note.
- No Dodgers or LA team marks in our design. The dyLAn hat's LA monogram belongs to the product photos only (trademark risk, flagged in 03).
- Who is in the photos: no names for people other than dylAn, and nobody identified as dylAn unless the source says so.
